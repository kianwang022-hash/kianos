Status: READY SOURCE REVIEW

- infer speaking from reading scores;

## S2｜ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines 2024

Sources:
https://www.actfl.org/proficiency-guidelines-overview
https://www.actfl.org/uploads/files/general/Resources-Publications/ACTFL_Proficiency_Guidelines_2024.pdf

Accepted:
- proficiency is functional ability to accomplish communication objectives;
- listening, speaking, reading and writing are separate domains;
- real-world proficiency concerns spontaneous, non-rehearsed language use;
- functional ability can be described by functions/tasks, accuracy, context/content and text type.

Learner use:
- supports task-based baselines;
- supports separating domain evidence;
- supports moving from words / sentences toward sustained discourse.

Boundary:
- ACTFL levels are descriptive proficiency frameworks, not mandatory course sequences;
- no learner must take an ACTFL assessment for this Skill;
- copyright-sensitive descriptors should be referenced, not reproduced wholesale.

## S4｜Munro & Derwing — accent, comprehensibility and intelligibility

Sources:
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-1770.1995.tb00963.x
https://doi.org/10.1075/bct.121.02mun

Accepted:
- accentedness, comprehensibility and intelligibility are related but partially independent;
- strongly accented L2 speech can still be highly intelligible;
- pronunciation goals should distinguish listener understanding from degree of foreign accent.

Learner use:
- supports intelligibility-first pronunciation;
- prevents native-accent mythology.

Boundary:
- findings do not imply pronunciation does not matter;
- intelligibility depends on speaker, listener, task and context;
- specific pronunciation priorities must come from actual listener / task evidence.

## Durable boundaries

### Exam English is evidence for only the tested capability

Reading accuracy, vocabulary tests and exam objective scores can be strong evidence for those domains.

They do not by themselves establish:
- spontaneous speaking;
- pronunciation;
- listening under natural conditions;
- live interaction;
- professional discussion.

### Native-like accent is not the target

The first pronunciation targets are:
- intelligibility;
- comprehensibility;
- stable production;
- listener effort appropriate to the task.

Accent reduction can be an optional preference, not a universal success condition.

### Fluency is not “speak as fast as possible”

Useful fluency includes:
- timely retrieval;
- sustainable pace;
- chunking;
- turn management;
- repair;
- enough accuracy to preserve intended meaning.

### Grammar perfection is not a gate to speaking

Some errors matter because they change meaning or credibility.

Others can coexist with successful communication.

Repair by impact, not by treating every deviation as equally urgent.

### Passive input is necessary but not sufficient for productive ability

Reading and listening can expand knowledge and pattern familiarity.

Speaking / writing performance still requires production and retrieval practice.

### Cross-cultural competence is calibration, not stereotype memory

Country / culture generalizations may generate hypotheses.

They should never override evidence from the actual person, institution and situation.

### AI practice is not final proof

AI can provide high-volume rehearsal and feedback.

When the target itself is human interaction, final evidence must include unpredictable real human interaction. Reading, writing or information-access goals can use domain-appropriate evidence instead.
