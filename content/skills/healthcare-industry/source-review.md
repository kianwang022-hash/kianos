## S2｜FDA — drug development and approval lifecycle

Sources:
https://www.fda.gov/patients/learn-about-drug-and-device-approvals/drug-development-process
https://www.fda.gov/patients/drug-development-process/step-3-clinical-research
https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-approval-process-drugs
https://www.fda.gov/patients/drug-development-process/step-4-fda-drug-review
https://www.fda.gov/patients/drug-development-process/step-5-fda-post-market-drug-safety-monitoring

Accepted:
- a useful U.S. lifecycle view is discovery/development → preclinical → clinical research → review → post-market monitoring;
- clinical phases are designed to answer different development questions;
- FDA approval is a benefit-risk decision for an intended population and use, based on submitted evidence and uncertainty;
- labeling and risk management are part of the regulatory product;
- evidence continues to evolve after approval.

Boundary:
- FDA phases / IND / NDA / BLA are U.S.-specific regulatory structures;
- phase number does not by itself describe study quality, decision importance or commercial value;
- approval does not mean zero risk or universal appropriateness.

## S4｜FDA — benefit-risk and lifecycle risk management

Sources:
https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/benefit-risk-assessment-new-drug-and-biological-products
https://www.fda.gov/industry/prescription-drug-user-fee-amendments/enhancing-benefit-risk-assessment-regulatory-decision-making
https://www.fda.gov/drugs/risk-evaluation-and-mitigation-strategies-rems/fdas-role-managing-medication-risks

Accepted:
- benefit-risk assessment integrates evidence, uncertainty, target condition, alternatives and risk-management options;
- patient experience data may inform benefit-risk;
- risk management is iterative across the lifecycle;
- labeling is a major risk-management tool and some products require additional measures.

Boundary:
- exact FDA frameworks are jurisdiction-specific;
- a favorable regulatory benefit-risk decision is not the same as payer value, clinician preference or investor return.

## S5｜FDA / ICH — RWD and RWE

Sources:
https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/considerations-use-real-world-data-and-real-world-evidence-support-regulatory-decision-making-drug
https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/real-world-data-assessing-electronic-health-records-and-medical-claims-data-support-regulatory
https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/m14-general-principles-planning-designing-analyzing-and-reporting-non-interventional-studies-utilize
https://www.fda.gov/science-research/real-world-evidence/fda-use-real-world-evidence-regulatory-decision-making

Accepted:
- RWD are routinely collected health / healthcare data; RWE is clinical evidence generated from analysis of RWD;
- RWE can support selected regulatory decisions across the product lifecycle;
- data relevance / reliability, study design, analysis and transparency matter;
- ICH M14 final guidance was issued by FDA in March 2026 for non-interventional RWD safety studies.

Boundary:
- “real world” does not mean low bias or high external validity automatically;
- large databases cannot rescue poor measurement, confounding or wrong time alignment;
- exact regulatory acceptability is purpose- and jurisdiction-specific.

## S6｜EMA — pharmacovigilance / post-authorisation

Sources:
https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/pharmacovigilance-overview
https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/post-authorisation/pharmacovigilance-post-authorisation
https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/pharmacovigilance-post-authorisation/post-authorisation-measures-questions-and-answers

Accepted:
- pre-authorisation trials leave uncertainty because populations and follow-up are limited;
- pharmacovigilance continues safety monitoring after authorisation;
- risk-management plans, signal management, post-authorisation safety / efficacy work and regulatory actions are lifecycle activities;
- marketing-authorisation holders have ongoing responsibilities.

Boundary:
- EMA rules are EU-specific;
- adverse-event collection is only one part of pharmacovigilance;
- a safety signal is not automatically a proven causal adverse reaction.

## S7｜NICE — HTA / health economics / value

Sources:
https://www.nice.org.uk/process/pmg36/chapter/introduction-to-health-technology-evaluation
https://www.nice.org.uk/process/pmg36/chapter/evidence-2/
https://www.nice.org.uk/process/pmg36/chapter/economic-evaluation-2/
https://www.nice.org.uk/what-nice-does/our-guidance/about-technology-appraisal-guidance/what-is-technology-appraisal-guidance

Accepted:
- technology appraisal combines clinical evidence with cost effectiveness and broader decision considerations;
- cost-utility analysis can compare costs with health effects measured in QALYs;
- evidence and economic modelling must make uncertainty visible;
- a product may be clinically effective yet still face separate system-level value / access decisions.

Boundary:
- NICE methods and thresholds are UK/NHS-specific and change over time;
- QALY / ICER are decision tools, not universal measures of human value;
- HTA is broader than price negotiation.

Boundary:

## Durable boundaries

### The product lifecycle is a decision chain, not a phase checklist

Every stage should reduce specific uncertainty:
- mechanism;
- safety;
- dose / exposure;
- efficacy / benefit;
- comparative value;
- manufacturing / quality;
- real-world safety;
- access.

“Phase 3” alone says little about whether a program is attractive.

### Approval is not success

Approval can coexist with:
- narrow label;
- safety burden;
- weak differentiation;
- poor reimbursement;
- operational complexity;
- low uptake;
- commercial failure.

### Regulatory systems are jurisdiction-specific

Use FDA / NMPA / EMA as concrete examples.

Never teach one regulator’s filing names, timelines or pathways as universal.

### RWD is raw material; RWE is an inference product

Dataset size does not establish:
- measurement validity;
- comparability;
- confounding control;
- correct time alignment;
- causal identification.

### Pharmacovigilance is active lifecycle risk management

It includes:
- case / signal detection;
- aggregate review;
- literature;
- RMP / risk minimization;
- post-authorisation studies;
- communication and regulatory action.

Do not reduce it to collecting adverse-event reports.

### Market Access is not just negotiation

Access can depend on:
- clinical evidence;
- comparative value;
- economic evidence;
- budget;
- coverage rules;
- formulary / pathway;
- implementation burden;
- eligibility;
- negotiation.

### HEOR is not “prove the drug saves money”

Its scientific role is to estimate:
- outcomes;
- resource use;
- costs;
- patient value / quality of life;
- comparative / economic consequences;
with uncertainty visible.

### QALY / ICER are tools, not moral truth

They can support transparent resource-allocation analysis in some systems.

They do not replace:
- equity;
- severity;
- uncertainty;
- feasibility;
- legal / societal values;
- other system-specific modifiers.

### Medical Affairs is not automatically promotion

But scientific intent does not exempt a communication from law / policy.

The content, audience, context, labeling, solicitation and local rule all matter.

### Industry analysis must connect science to economics

A compelling mechanism or huge market does not automatically produce:
- clinical benefit;
- approval;
- access;
- durable differentiation;
- sustainable margins.
