# Experience / Leisure / Hobbies Source Review

Status: READY SOURCE REVIEW
Role: whole-book leisure-value / motivation / recovery-interface / attention-boundary review for experience-hobbies.

This Skill manages an optional interest portfolio.

It must not become:
- productivity colonization of leisure;
- a requirement that hobbies create assets;
- a recovery system competing with Health;
- endless formal curricula;
- gear acquisition as identity;
- moral panic about gaming / screens;
- denial of meaningful functional harm;
- social-status hobby collection.

Source:
kian-personal-os/SKILLS.md — Experience, leisure & compound hobbies.

## S2｜Self-Determination Theory — intrinsic motivation

Sources:
https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/the-theory/
https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/topics/application-intrinsic-motivation/

Accepted:
- intrinsic motivation concerns doing an activity for inherent interest / enjoyment;
- autonomy, competence and relatedness are central constructs in SDT;
- contexts can support or undermine sustained high-quality motivation;
- extrinsic reward / evaluation can change the quality of motivation.

Boundary:
- SDT is a broad motivation theory, not a hobby-ranking algorithm;
- an activity does not need to satisfy all three needs to be worth doing;
- enjoyment can be legitimate without deliberate mastery.

---

## S3｜Leisure engagement and subjective well-being meta-analysis

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25602273/

Accepted:
- leisure engagement / satisfaction is associated with subjective well-being across multiple evidence designs;
- leisure satisfaction is a meaningful outcome rather than merely a route to productivity.

Boundary:
- much of the literature includes observational evidence;
- individual activity choice, causality and effect size vary;
- this does not prove that any specific hobby improves well-being for every person.

---

## S4｜Psychological detachment from work — systematic review

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37141024/

Accepted:
- psychological detachment from work is an important recovery experience in occupational-health research;
- work / communication technology and boundary conditions can interfere with detachment;
- leisure can sometimes create context for detachment.

Boundary:
- detachment is not the whole recovery system;
- hobby participation is not required for recovery;
- Health remains owner of recovery selection and dose.

## S5｜WHO — arts and health

Sources:
https://www.who.int/europe/publications/i/item/9789289054553
https://www.who.int/initiatives/arts-and-health

Accepted:
- a large multidisciplinary evidence base links arts engagement with health / well-being processes across prevention, promotion and illness contexts;
- artistic activity can carry emotional, social, communicative and experiential value.

Boundary:
- the 2019 review includes heterogeneous designs and cannot turn an art hobby into a universal treatment;
- arts participation in this Skill is leisure / creative capability, not medical therapy.

---

## S6｜WHO — gaming disorder boundary

Sources:
https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/gaming-disorder
https://www.who.int/health-topics/addictive-behaviour

Accepted:
- gaming disorder is defined by impaired control, increasing priority over other activities and continuation / escalation despite negative consequences, with significant functional impairment;
- WHO explicitly notes that gaming disorder affects only a small proportion of people who game;
- gaming should therefore not be moralized as inherently harmful, while meaningful impairment must not be ignored.

Boundary:
- this Skill does not diagnose gaming disorder;
- high play time alone is not a diagnosis;
- distress / impairment or loss of control belongs to Health / professional evaluation.

## Durable boundaries

### Enjoyment is a legitimate endpoint

A hobby does not need:
- monetization;
- public output;
- career relevance;
- measurable skill growth;
- recovery proof.

### Compounding is a bonus, not admission requirement

Some hobbies become richer with years of skill and taste.

Others remain occasional enjoyment.

Both are valid.

### Recovery does not need to produce an asset

Health owns recovery.

A leisure activity may help recovery even if it creates no durable skill or artifact.

### Hobby identity can replace hobby behavior

Buying, watching reviews, following communities and talking about gear can consume more attention than doing the activity.

Track actual use.

### Skill growth can destroy the original leisure value

If deliberate practice turns a loved hobby into another performance obligation, it is legitimate to step back to casual play.

### Social value must be real

A hobby community can create:
- friendship;
- belonging;
- shared practice.

It should not be forced into career networking.

### Novelty is not portfolio evidence

New interests often feel unusually exciting.

Sample cheaply before buying infrastructure or creating a curriculum.

### Gaming is neither automatically harmful nor automatically harmless

Look at:
- control;
- displacement;
- sleep;
- physical health;
- finances;
- relationships;
- study / work function.

### Gear should follow use

Upgrade when current gear is a demonstrated constraint on the experience or skill.

Do not make acquisition the hobby.

### Hobbies need an attention budget

The full cost includes:
- activity;
- content about activity;
- shopping;
- setup;
- maintenance;
- travel;
- community;
- recovery cost.
