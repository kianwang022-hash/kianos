## S2｜Listening and listener perception

Sources:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11978453/
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4270393/

Accepted:
- people perceived as good listeners are associated with more positive interpersonal judgments;
- active-listening cues can change the speaker's social / emotional experience;
- listening is relational behavior, not only information extraction.

Boundary:
- listening effects depend on context;
- visible listening behaviors are not proof of genuine understanding;
- eye contact / nodding / paraphrasing should not become ritual checklists.

---

## S3｜Conversation repair

Source:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6849777/

Accepted:
- ordinary conversation contains mechanisms for detecting and repairing problems in speaking, hearing and understanding;
- communication quality includes noticing misunderstanding and repairing it, not merely producing a clear first message.

Boundary:
- conversation-analysis descriptions do not create a universal script for every relationship or culture.

## S4｜Harvard Program on Negotiation — BATNA / reservation point

Sources:
https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/batna/translate-your-batna-to-the-current-deal/
https://www.pon.harvard.edu/tag/reservation-price/
https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/business-negotiations/negotiation-preparation-strategies/

Accepted:
- BATNA means the realistic alternative if no agreement is reached;
- reservation point / value is tied to the value of alternatives;
- negotiation preparation should include alternatives, targets, walk-away conditions and counterpart constraints;
- multi-issue negotiation can create room for trade-offs.

Boundary:
- PON is practitioner / academic educational guidance, not proof that BATNA alone determines outcomes;
- BATNA estimates can be wrong or change;
- bluffing / deception can damage credibility and may cross ethical or legal boundaries.

---

## S5｜Conflict communication is context-dependent

Source:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5181851/

Accepted:
- direct vs cooperative communication can have different effects depending on problem severity and the counterpart's ability / willingness to respond;
- there is no universal “always soften” or “always confront” conflict style.

Boundary:
- evidence includes intimate-relationship contexts and should not be mechanically generalized to all workplace / professional conflicts;
- high-risk harassment, coercion, violence or formal misconduct may require escalation rather than interpersonal technique.

---

## S6｜Trust repair and apology

Sources:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6349725/
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8994847/

Accepted:
- trust violations can require repair rather than simple reassurance;
- apology can include acknowledgment, responsibility, explanation, repair and future commitment;
- empathy / affective response can influence trust repair.

Boundary:
- apology is not a guaranteed repair mechanism;
- the right response depends on what actually happened, responsibility and evidence;
- serious misconduct is not erased by persuasive apology.

---

## S7｜Weak ties and network structure

Sources:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-022-00296-z
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-021-01196-4.pdf

Accepted:
- weak ties can connect otherwise separated parts of a network and support information flow;
- work structure can affect formation / loss of weak ties;
- network structure changes under remote / hybrid work.

Boundary:
- network value is context-dependent;
- weak ties do not imply “collect as many acquaintances as possible”;
- relationships have intrinsic human value beyond opportunity flow.

---

## S8｜Feedback conversations

Source:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7304587/

Accepted:
- feedback conversations can become counterproductive;
- performance feedback needs to support future improvement rather than only adjudicate the past;
- disagreement between giver and receiver is itself information.

Boundary:
- the cited studies are mainly work / performance contexts;
- no single feedback template is universally optimal.

## Durable boundaries

### Charisma is not communication quality

Fluency, confidence, speed and social ease can improve delivery.

They do not guarantee:
- truth;
- understanding;
- judgment;
- trustworthiness.

### Listening is not a performance checklist

Good listening should change the listener's model.

Nodding and paraphrasing without real updating are theater.

### Persuasion must preserve agency

Do not teach:
- deception;
- concealed material information;
- manufactured urgency;
- exploiting vulnerability;
- coercive dependency.

The durable target is better understanding and voluntary agreement.

### BATNA is not a magic number

Alternatives can be uncertain, multi-dimensional and change over time.

Reservation values should be grounded in real comparable options, not invented leverage.

### Conflict style is conditional

Some problems need warmth and repair.

Some need explicit disagreement, accountability or boundaries.

Some should leave the interpersonal channel and move to formal escalation.

### Trust is not blind optimism

Trust can differ by:
- competence;
- reliability;
- integrity;
- confidentiality;
- incentives;
- domain.

A person may deserve trust in one role and not another.

### Networking must not instrumentalize people

A network is not a leaderboard or contact database.

No learner requirement should force:
- relationship logging;
- touch-frequency quotas;
- transactional “value extraction”;
- fake intimacy.
