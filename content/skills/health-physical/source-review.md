## S2｜CDC + AASM — sleep duration and quality

Sources:
https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/
https://aasm.org/clinical-resources/practice-standards/consensus-statements-and-papers/
https://aasm.org/resources/pdf/pressroom/adult-sleep-duration-consensus.pdf

Accepted:
- adequate sleep and sleep quality are basic health inputs;
- sleep need varies by age;
- CDC lists 7+ hours for adults 18–60, with age-specific ranges beyond that;
- AASM / SRS consensus recommends 7 or more hours regularly for healthy adults;
- sleep health is broader than duration alone.

Boundary:
- 7 hours is a population-level floor, not a personalized optimum;
- sleep timing, regularity, quality, disorders and daytime function matter;
- persistent insomnia, excessive sleepiness, snoring / breathing concern or functional impairment can require professional evaluation;
- wearable sleep stages are not treated as diagnostic truth.

## S3｜HHS + CDC — physical activity

Sources:
https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines/current-guidelines/top-10-things-know
https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/physical-activity/

Accepted:
- adults should move more and sit less;
- current U.S. public-health guidance uses at least 150–300 minutes moderate aerobic activity or equivalent vigorous activity for substantial health benefit;
- muscle-strengthening activity on at least 2 days per week is part of the adult guideline;
- physical activity has health benefits beyond weight change.

Boundary:
- these are population health targets, not individualized athletic programming;
- training volume / intensity must account for baseline, goals, recovery, injury and medical context;
- more exercise is not always better.

## S4｜WHO Healthy Diet — January 2026

Source:
https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet

Accepted:
- healthy diets can take many forms;
- WHO organizes healthy diet around adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity;
- minimally processed / unprocessed foods and limiting unhealthy fats, free sugars and excess sodium are durable public-health directions;
- food safety is part of healthy diet.

Boundary:
- one cultural diet pattern is not universally required;
- individual disease, pregnancy, allergy, eating-disorder, renal / metabolic or medication contexts may require individualized professional advice;
- supplement use is not assumed necessary for everyone.

## S5｜CDC / NIDDK — sustainable weight change

Sources:
https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/losing-weight/
https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/body-weight-planner
https://www.niddk.nih.gov/research-funding/at-niddk/labs-branches/laboratory-biological-modeling/integrative-physiology-section/research/body-weight-planner

Accepted:
- sustained weight change is influenced by energy intake, activity and dynamic physiologic adaptation;
- gradual, sustainable lifestyle change is preferred over crash strategies;
- sleep, stress, medication, health conditions, environment and age can affect weight management;
- static calorie arithmetic can misrepresent long-term body-weight dynamics.

Boundary:
- scale weight is not the same as body composition or health;
- no fixed calorie deficit / weekly loss target is universal;
- unexplained or rapid weight change belongs to clinical evaluation;
- this Skill does not prescribe weight-loss medication or restrictive diets.

## Durable boundaries

### Health tracking is not clinical diagnosis

Consumer wearables, smart scales and apps can be useful for trends.

They do not independently diagnose sleep disorders, arrhythmia, overtraining, endocrine disease or body-composition pathology.

### Recovery is not a gadget category

High-value recovery inputs are often basic:
- sleep;
- sufficient nutrition;
- appropriate training load;
- stress / workload reduction;
- movement / active recovery;
- time.

Devices or supplements must earn their marginal value.

### Sleep duration is not the only sleep outcome

Track:
- daytime function;
- regularity;
- quality;
- schedule fit;
- persistent symptoms.

### Diet quality and body composition are separate but interacting goals

A calorie deficit can reduce weight with poor food quality.

A high-quality diet can still be mismatched to a specific body-composition goal.

Both matter.

### Body composition is not worth unlimited attention

Weight / appearance goals should not consume health, social function, cognitive performance, relationship with food or training recovery.

### Physical activity guideline is a floor, not a competition

Use population guidance as orientation.

Progression should reflect current capacity and target performance.

### Pain is information, not a badge of effort

Training discomfort and delayed muscle soreness differ from acute injury, neurologic symptoms, persistent / escalating pain or meaningful functional loss.

When uncertainty is high or function deteriorates, escalate.

### Prevention is current-first

Vaccination and screening schedules change.

Use current professional / official recommendations for age, risk and jurisdiction.

### There is no single perfect posture

Comfort, adjustability, task variation and movement are more durable than freezing one visual posture.

### Appearance maintenance should remain bounded

Health protection, hygiene, grooming and fit can improve daily life.

If appearance management becomes compulsive, unsafe or disproportionately attention-consuming, the correct action is not endless optimization.
