## S2｜CFPB — emergency savings and cash-flow resilience

Sources:
https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/
https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/emergency-savings-financial-security-insights-from-making-ends-meet-survey-and-consumer-credit-panel/

Accepted:
- cash-flow timing affects short-term financial resilience;
- emergency savings can help absorb income / expense shocks;
- financial constraints can impede saving, so reserve design cannot be reduced to one universal number.

Boundary:
- no universal “3 / 6 / 12 month” reserve rule is promoted as law;
- exact reserve need depends on income stability, obligations, liquidity and available backstops.

## S3｜CFA Institute — IPS, risk capacity and constraints

Sources:
https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/basics-of-portfolio-planning-and-construction
https://www.cfainstitute.org/standards/professionals/code-ethics-standards/standards-of-practice-iii-c

Accepted:
- a written investment policy can connect objectives with risk and constraints;
- willingness to take risk and ability / capacity to take risk are different;
- important constraints include liquidity, time horizon, taxes, legal / regulatory factors and unique circumstances;
- portfolio construction should follow objectives and constraints rather than begin with securities.

Boundary:
- CFA material is professional curriculum / standards, not a personalized allocation algorithm;
- exact tax / legal constraints are jurisdiction- and person-specific.

## S4｜Investor.gov / FINRA — asset allocation, diversification and rebalancing

Sources:
https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/asset-allocation
https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/general-resources/publications-research/info-sheets/beginners-guide-asset

Accepted:
- asset allocation spreads portfolio exposure across asset categories;
- diversification operates across and within asset classes;
- diversification can reduce concentration risk but cannot eliminate investment loss;
- market moves can shift a portfolio away from target allocation;
- rebalancing restores target risk exposure and may involve costs / taxes.

Boundary:
- no universal stock / bond ratio;
- no universal rebalancing calendar or threshold;
- correlation changes with market conditions and historical correlation is not a guarantee.

## S5｜FINRA — bond risks and duration

Source:
https://syndication.finra.org/content/bond-investing-and-due-diligence
https://syndication.finra.org/content/duration-what-interest-rate-hike-could-do-your-bond-portfolio

Accepted:
- bond investors face interest-rate, credit, liquidity, call and reinvestment risks among others;
- duration is a measure related to price sensitivity to interest-rate changes;
- longer / higher-duration exposure can be more sensitive to rate changes.

Boundary:
- duration is an approximation / sensitivity measure, not a complete bond-risk score;
- individual bonds and bond funds can behave differently;
- credit, liquidity, call, convexity and other risks are not captured by duration alone;
- low duration does not mean low overall risk.

## S6｜Investor.gov — fees and performance claims

Sources:
https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/updated
https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-47

Accepted:
- fees reduce the amount of capital compounding for the investor;
- small recurring fee differences can compound into meaningful long-run differences;
- past performance does not necessarily predict future results;
- performance presentations require scrutiny of calculation, period, benchmark and omitted costs.

Boundary:
- low fee does not automatically make an unsuitable product suitable;
- higher cost is not automatically unjustified when services / exposures genuinely differ.

## Durable boundaries

### Return is not the first variable

The first question is whether capital can survive required withdrawals, liabilities and shocks.

A portfolio with attractive expected return can still be inappropriate if it creates forced selling at the wrong time.

### Risk is multidimensional

Volatility, duration, drawdown and questionnaire scores are each partial measurements, not “risk itself”.

A useful number can improve a decision without being allowed to decide the portfolio by itself.

Relevant risks can include:
- permanent loss;
- drawdown;
- liquidity;
- inflation;
- interest rates;
- credit;
- leverage;
- concentration;
- currency;
- sequence / timing;
- legal / operational failure.

### Diversification is not product count

Ten funds can still share the same companies, regions, duration or factor exposure.

### Historical return is not expected return

Backward-looking performance can inform history.

It does not create a reliable forward return estimate by itself.

### Valuation is conditional

Valuation exposes assumptions about future cash flows, growth, discount rates, terminal value and risk.

It does not produce one objectively certain fair value, and adding more decimal places does not make uncertain assumptions more certain.

### Fees, taxes and friction are part of return

Compare outcomes after the costs that actually apply.

Tax rules and account structures are jurisdiction-specific and current-first.

### Do not use this book for market timing

No durable unit should teach:
- next-year index targets;
- tactical calls based on current headlines;
- “cash now because crash is coming”;
- “all-in because a bull market is starting”.

Those are current decision / research tasks, not permanent capability rules.

### Professional advice needs due diligence

A title such as adviser / consultant / banker is not enough.

Current credentials, registration, compensation, conflicts, scope and fiduciary / legal duties must be checked in the relevant jurisdiction.
